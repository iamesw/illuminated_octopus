How's that journaling practice working out? Well Bob, it's not great. Somehow a month's gone by without writing. I could blame the dog going back to a normal schedule and not sitting on the porch at 5am with nothing to do, but it's like all habits. Without setting time aside, it gets lost.

And now? Total cliche. I'm without a device. It's as easy as that. 

I left my phone in the hotel room. All the tropes about staring at devices, but here I am, with my thoughts, a cup of coffee, pen, and notebook. Meanwhile, half the people in the coffee shop are staring at screens, or have them nearby, sitting on the table near their hands, competing for attention with their cup of coffee or croissant. 

Is it really that bad? Does it really matter? I'm only in this state because I stepped our of the hotel, realized I'd left my phone, thought, fuck it, I know where the coffee shop is, why not go on? I'd also serendipitously put my small journal in my back pocket already, so there was a bit of stage setting. Planning? Dare I say intention setting?

There's that pesky thought again. Thinking about my internal aversion to "setting intention," I am a designer that sets about every new project with intention—or at least a concept. A goal in mind. Yet with the smaller stuff in life I want to take a backseat, or maybe some of the large things as well. 

I'm approaching twenty years at the museum as much through inertia as anything else. A decade ago I fell in love with Berlin, joked about trying to emigrate. It remains a fleeting idea. I've considered leaving the museum, but never have. 

But now, I find myself at a coffee shop in Baltimore, attending a conference on my own dime to network and seek out clients for freelance work. To follow through on an intention set long ago to leave the museum. Finding a way to stay in the field, but get back to the things I enjoy making. 

It scares me though. Perhaps some of the lack of intentionality in life is as simple as not failing. If there isn't a goal to achieve, there's no failure in not achieving it. I've also had a deep aversion to striving and hustle culture, so it's easy to fall into stasis.
